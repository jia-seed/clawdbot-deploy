## Page 2: Your Minimum Viable Offer

Before you reach out to anyone, answer these:

**1. What do you do?** (One sentence)
> "I design landing pages that convert visitors into customers."

**2. Who is it for?** (Specific)
> "SaaS startups launching new features"

**3. What's the outcome?** (Tangible)
> "A high-converting landing page delivered in 5 days"

**Fill in yours:**
- I [verb] [deliverable] that [outcome]
- For [specific audience]
- Delivered in [timeframe]

**Don't skip this.** Vague offers get vague responses.

## Page 3: The Pricing Formula

**Stop guessing. Use this:**

```
Your Rate = (Desired Monthly Income ÷ 20 billable hours) × 1.5

Example: $5,000/month goal
$5,000 ÷ 20 = $250/hour
$250 × 1.5 = $375/hour (buffer for taxes, tools, slow months)
```

**Project pricing:**
- Estimate hours honestly
- Multiply by your rate
- Add 20% buffer
- Round to a clean number

**The confidence script:**
> "My rate for this project is $X. That includes [deliverables] delivered by [date]. Want me to send over a proposal?"

Say it out loud 10 times. You need to believe it.

## Page 6: The Daily Outreach System

**The 10-10-10 Method (30 mins/day):**

1. **10 minutes:** Find 10 potential clients using your hunting grounds
2. **10 minutes:** Personalize messages using templates
3. **10 minutes:** Send all 10, track in a simple spreadsheet

**Tracking spreadsheet columns:**
| Name | Platform | Message Sent | Date | Response | Follow-up Date | Status |

**Follow-up schedule:**
- No response after 3 days → Send follow-up #1
- No response after 7 days → Send follow-up #2
- No response after 14 days → Move on

**Follow-up template:**
```
Hey [Name], just floating this back up. Still interested in chatting about [service]? No worries if the timing isn't right.
```

## Page 7: The Discovery Call Script

**Goal:** Understand their problem, establish fit, quote a price.

**The 5 Questions:**

1. "Tell me about your business and what you're working on right now."
   *(Let them talk. Take notes.)*

2. "What made you respond to my message?"
   *(Reveals their pain point)*

3. "What have you tried so far?"
   *(Shows sophistication level)*

4. "What would success look like for this project?"
   *(Gets them to define value)*

5. "What's your timeline and budget range?"
   *(If they won't answer, give a range: "Projects like this typically run $X-Y")*

**Closing:**
> "Based on what you've shared, I think I can help. Here's what I'm thinking: [brief solution]. I'll send over a proposal by [date] with the details and pricing. Sound good?"

## Page 9: The Anti-Scope-Creep Contract

**Add these clauses to every agreement:**

### Revisions Clause
> "This project includes [X] rounds of revisions. Additional revisions are billed at $[hourly rate] per hour."

### Scope Change Clause  
> "Any work outside the agreed deliverables will be quoted separately before beginning."

### Timeline Clause
> "Timeline assumes client provides feedback within [X] business days. Delays in feedback extend the delivery date accordingly."

### Kill Fee Clause
> "If the project is cancelled after kickoff, a minimum of [X]% of the project fee is due for work completed."

**The magic words when scope creeps:**
> "Happy to do that! It's outside our original scope, so let me send over a quick quote for the addition."

Say it friendly. Say it every time.
